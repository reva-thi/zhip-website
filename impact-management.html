Impact Management

Measured with precision.
Reported with honesty.
Improved continuously.

ZHIP's impact management system exists because communities, donors, partners, and the public deserve to know — in plain language, with verified numbers — whether what we are doing is working. We measure everything that matters, report it transparently, and use it to do better.

What impact management means at ZHIP

Not just counting meals. Understanding change.

Impact management is more than counting meals served. A number of meals served tells you something. But it does not tell you whether the people eating those meals are less food insecure than they were six months ago. It does not tell you whether the family that joined a cooking class has changed how they eat. It does not tell you whether the child who planted tomatoes at age eight will grow up with a fundamentally different relationship to food.

ZHIP tracks both outputs — what we did — and outcomes — what changed as a result. Output data is collected at every programme session. Outcome data is collected through regular check-ins with programme participants, partner healthcare data, and community wellbeing assessments. The combination gives us — and you — a genuine picture of whether we are making the difference we set out to make.

When the data tells us something is not working — and it does, sometimes — we change it. Impact management is not about producing reports that look good. It is about having the information we need to do better for the communities we serve.

"We started tracking whether families who received food boxes were still receiving them after six months. The data showed that 40% had reduced or stopped collecting — because they had started growing more of their own food and cooking more from scratch. That is the outcome we are really after. The box is a bridge, not a destination."

— ZHIP Impact Team

How data flows through the system

From session to dashboard — in real time.

1

Data captured at the point of activity

Volunteers and programme leads record data in the ZHIP app during or immediately after every session — meals served, attendances, deliveries completed, food quantities. This is integrated into normal programme workflows: it takes less than two minutes per session. Data quality is the hub coordinator's responsibility.

2

Automated aggregation and SDG mapping

The ZHIP platform aggregates data automatically — summing across sessions, hubs, programmes, and time periods. It maps every data point to the relevant UN SDG target using ZHIP's standard SDG framework. Hub coordinators do not need to understand the SDG framework — the platform handles all mapping automatically.

3

Real-time public dashboard updated

The ZHIP public impact dashboard is updated in real time as data is submitted. Meals served today. Kgs rescued this week. Certifications issued this month. The dashboard is publicly accessible — no login required — and embeddable in partner websites.

4

Quarterly impact report generation

Every quarter, the ZHIP platform auto-generates an impact report for each hub, each regional association, and the ZHIP platform as a whole. The report is written in plain language by the ZHIP impact team using the automated data — no raw numbers, no jargon, just a clear narrative of what happened and what it meant.

5

Personalised donor impact updates

Each donor receives a quarterly personalised impact update — specific to the programme and hub they funded — showing exactly what their support produced. Not a generic thank-you. A specific, honest account of meals cooked, people reached, and change observed.

6

Annual Benefit Report — independent verification

Annually, ZHIP's aggregate impact data is submitted to an independent assessor for verification against the B Impact Assessment standard. The verified report is published publicly — accessible to all stakeholders, shareholders, and the general public. Any areas where ZHIP did not meet its targets are reported openly alongside the areas where targets were met or exceeded.

7

Continuous improvement — data informing decisions

Impact data is reviewed by the ZHIP programme team monthly and by the board quarterly. Where data reveals a programme is underperforming — lower meal counts than target, lower certification rates, poor volunteer retention — the team investigates root causes and proposes adjustments. Programme decisions are data-informed, not data-driven: the numbers guide human judgement, they do not replace it.

Reporting for different audiences

The right information for the right person — every time.

👤 For Donors

Personalised quarterly impact statement: what you funded, specific outcomes in the programmes you supported, one human story connected to your contribution, and a clear summary of how ZHIP's finances are managed. Plain language. Honest about limitations. Grateful without being sycophantic.

Format: personalised PDF + platform dashboard. Frequency: quarterly.

🏛️ For Organisational Partners

Custom impact report aligned to the partner's own reporting frameworks — SDG alignment for NGOs, ESG metrics for corporate partners, clinical outcome data for healthcare partners, educational attainment indicators for school partners. Co-branded where appropriate for partner use in their own stakeholder reporting.

Format: co-branded PDF report. Frequency: quarterly or per-event.

📊 For Investors

Full financial and impact transparency: revenue by stream, expenditure by programme, community food fund disbursements, platform growth metrics, and the annual B Impact Assessment score. Financial statements audited by an independent accountant. Impact verified by B Lab assessor.

Format: annual report + quarterly updates. Frequency: annual formal, quarterly progress.

🌐 For the Public

The real-time public impact dashboard — always accessible, no login required. Live meal count, live food rescue totals, live certification numbers, live hub count. Updated as programme sessions are logged. The most transparent possible account of what ZHIP is doing, for anyone who wants to see it.

Format: public web dashboard. Frequency: real-time.

🏠 For Hub Coordinators

Hub-level operational dashboard — programme attendance trends, volunteer hours, food handled, client reach, certification completions, and month-over-month comparison. Helps coordinators identify what is working, what is declining, and where to focus effort in the coming weeks.

Format: real-time hub dashboard. Frequency: real-time, reviewed weekly.

🏛️ For Government and Regulators

SDG-formatted impact reports compatible with government outcome frameworks. Texas PBC Annual Benefit Report. Anonymised, aggregated community health data for public health reporting partnerships. Grant compliance reports for USDA, state, and local government funders.

Format: formatted government reports. Frequency: as required by funder or regulator.

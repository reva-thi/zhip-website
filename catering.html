Food Preparation & Catering

From prep to plate —
every detail that
makes the meal possible.

Excellent food does not happen by accident. It happens through careful planning, trained hands, organised logistics, and the collective effort of a volunteer team that knows exactly what they are doing and why. This is how ZHIP prepares and delivers every meal — and how the ZHIP catering service extends that capability to events, organisations, and community celebrations.

Food preparation — the operations

What happens in the hub kitchen — every preparation day.

Weekly menu planning

Every hub kitchen plans its menu one week in advance — but that menu is always provisional, because the greatest variable is what food rescue brings in each day. The kitchen lead balances planned menus against available rescued ingredients, adapting in real time while maintaining nutritional standards.

Fri

Weekly planning session

Kitchen lead and hub coordinator review next week's commitments — how many meals at each service, any special dietary requirements from the senior and children's delivery lists, cultural events that require specific menus, available budget. A provisional seven-day menu is drafted.

Mon

Food rescue assessment

Monday morning's food rescue collection is assessed against the weekly menu plan. The kitchen lead adjusts accordingly — if a significant quantity of sweet potatoes arrived, Thursday's soup becomes a roasted sweet potato soup. If fresh herbs came in, they go into the Tuesday community kitchen. The menu is living, not fixed.

Daily

Daily production planning

The night before each kitchen session, the kitchen lead confirms: today's menu, ingredient quantities scaled to the session size, any allergen adaptations needed, assigned volunteer roles, preparation timeline from arrival to service, and packaging requirements for delivery programmes.

A kitchen preparation day — minute by minute

8:00 AM

Kitchen lead arrives. Temperature checks on refrigerated rescue food. Dry goods pulled from store. Today's menu confirmed. Equipment checked and sanitised.

8:30 AM

Volunteer team arrives. Kitchen lead assigns roles: prep team (washing, chopping, measuring), cooking team (hobs and oven), packing team (delivery and pantry distribution preparation).

9:00 AM

Preparation begins. Vegetables washed, peeled, diced. Grains measured and soaked or started. Stocks simmering. Temperature logs begun for any cooking below 75°C.

10:00 AM

Main cooking. Kitchen lead moves between stations — checking seasoning, adjusting heat, demonstrating techniques for new volunteers. Quality checks throughout.

11:00 AM

Delivery packing begins. Senior and children's delivery meals portioned, labelled with name, allergens, date, and reheating instructions. Temperature logged. Packed into insulated bags by route.

11:30 AM

Drivers collect their routes. Kitchen lead signs off each bag. Final temperature check logged. Service for community kitchen guests begins.

2:00 PM

Service ends. Surplus packaged for collection point. Kitchen cleaned to food safety standard. All records completed in ZHIP app. Impact data synced to platform.

Preparation by programme

How preparation adapts to each recipient group.

🧓 Senior Meals Preparation

🥄

Texture: Soft food option prepared separately for seniors with dental difficulties or swallowing conditions — same dish, blended or finely chopped, clearly labelled

🧂

Sodium: All senior meals are low-sodium by default. Higher-sodium versions prepared only for seniors with no hypertension history, confirmed at intake

🌡️

Temperature: Portioned into single-serve insulated containers. Temperature probe used to confirm every container leaves at or above 75°C

🏷️

Labelling: Name, date, dish name, key allergens, reheating instructions, and the volunteer's name — so the recipient knows who made their meal

🌍

Cultural adaptation: Where a senior's cultural food preferences are noted at intake, the kitchen lead ensures their meal reflects those preferences at least twice per week

👧 Children's Meal Preparation

🧒

Portion size: Age-appropriate portioning — smaller portions for under-5s, larger for teens. Each delivery note specifies age group and portion size

🥗

Nutrition priority: Children's meals contain at least two vegetable portions, one whole grain carbohydrate, and sufficient protein for growth — checked against the ZHIP children's nutrition standard

🎨

Presentation: Colourful presentation matters for children. The packing team uses colour in the plate composition where possible — orange sweet potato alongside green spinach, red tomato alongside yellow corn

⚠️

Allergen rigour: Children's meals require the highest allergen vigilance. Any allergen-free meal is prepared first, on a cleaned surface, before any common allergens are handled in the kitchen

📝

Activity inclusion: Weekly food box includes a recipe card the child can help with and a simple growing or food activity — reinforcing the connection between food and learning

👨‍👩‍👧 Family Kit Preparation

📦

Assembly: Monthly family food security kits assembled in a dedicated weekly session — a separate volunteer team from the kitchen, working to a standardised kit list with seasonal fresh additions

⚖️

Weighing and measuring: All dry goods weighed to a standard. Fresh produce selected for quality and quantity. Any items below standard removed. Every kit is the same quality.

🌱

Seed inclusion: Each kit includes three seed varieties — selected seasonally, appropriate for the time of year and a beginner grower. Seeds packaged with a growing guide card.

📋

Recipe cards: Five pictorial recipe cards per kit — different each month, using ingredients in the kit, suitable for the time of year, drawn from the cultural background noted for each family where possible

🏷️

Personalisation: Each kit is labelled with the family's name and includes any allergen substitutions agreed at intake. A handwritten note from the packing volunteer is encouraged.

ZHIP Catering Service

Community-cooked. Mission-funded. Extraordinarily good.

The ZHIP Catering Service is the social enterprise arm of the community kitchen programme — providing professional-quality catering for events and organisations, with 100% of net profit returning to the community food programmes that funded it.

What we cater

🏢

Corporate events — team lunches, away days, client dinners, product launches, and office catering. Seasonal menus, dietary accommodation, professional presentation, and the knowledge that your event directly funded community meals.

🎓

Educational institutions — school and university events, graduation celebrations, faculty dinners, and student orientation meals. Menu design informed by nutritional standards and student food culture research.

🕌

Faith community celebrations — Eid feasts, Christmas community dinners, Diwali celebrations, Passover seders, interfaith events, and harvest festivals. Menus co-designed with faith community leads to meet religious dietary requirements and cultural traditions.

🎉

Community celebrations — birthday parties, weddings, baby showers, graduation parties, community fundraisers, and cultural festivals. Full catering or meal-train-style contribution catering for community events.

🤝

Nonprofit and social enterprise events — conferences, gala dinners, fundraising events, and stakeholder meetings. Subsidised rates available for registered nonprofits. Impact reporting provided for ESG and CSR use.

🏛️

Government and public sector — public meetings, staff events, community consultations, and public health events where food is part of the programme. Community food sourcing and sustainability reporting provided.

Catering service tiers

Community Catering — $15 per head

For ZHIP hub events and registered community organisations. One hot dish, one salad, bread, and a non-alcoholic drink. Self-service format. Minimum 20 people. 100% of surplus goes back to the community food fund.

Standard Catering — $28 per head

Two hot dishes (including one plant-based), two salads, bread, dessert, and non-alcoholic drinks. Full allergen accommodation. Buffet or served format. Minimum 30 people. 40% of net profit to community food fund.

Celebration Catering — $45 per head

Full multi-course menu, culturally themed or bespoke, with full service staff from the hub. Includes a table card explaining the ZHIP story and the impact of each guest's attendance. Minimum 50 people. 35% of net profit to community food fund.

Corporate Impact Package — from $2,500

Full corporate event catering plus: ZHIP impact ambassador at the event to share the community food story, branded impact report for CSR use, and a matched community kitchen session funded from the event fee. The most complete integration of corporate giving and community food.

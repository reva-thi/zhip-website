Meal Preparation & Delivery

From the hub kitchen
to the door that
needs it most.

The ZHIP Meal Preparation and Delivery programme is the operational engine behind every meal that leaves a hub kitchen — a coordinated system of preparation, packaging, logistics, and delivery that ensures nutritious hot food reaches children, youth, and seniors safely, consistently, and with dignity.

Volunteer as a Driver Partner Your Kitchen
How it works — end to end

From planning to plate — every step organised.

📋

Plan

Weekly menus planned around: nutritional requirements of each recipient group, cultural preferences, available rescued food, seasonal produce from growing programme, and budget.

🍳

Prepare

Fresh preparation by the hub kitchen volunteer team — portioned, labelled with recipient name and dietary notes, temperature-checked, and packed in insulated delivery containers.

🚗

Deliver

Matched volunteer drivers collect routes from the hub, deliver within the safe temperature window (within 90 minutes of leaving the kitchen), and complete the welfare visit protocol at every door.

📊

Record

Every delivery logged in the ZHIP app — confirmed receipt, volunteer observations, any welfare concerns flagged. Data feeds the hub's real-time impact dashboard and monthly impact report.

Who receives meals and how

Three recipient groups. Three delivery models.

👧 Children & Youth

Daily hot meal delivery to homebound children and young people — those unable to access school meals, holiday clubs, or community kitchens due to disability, illness, or caring responsibilities.

DELIVERY DETAILS
⏰

Delivered daily, arriving within the family's agreed window (typically 11am–2pm)

📦

Hot meal + weekly supplementary food box with ingredients and activity kits

🤝

15-minute minimum visit from trained volunteer — welfare check protocol

🌍

Culturally appropriate menu — adapted to the child's dietary needs and preferences

📊

Monthly nutrition monitoring and dietary plan review by hub food lead

🧓 Seniors & Elders

Daily hot meal delivery to elderly and housebound adults — with a human visit at every delivery that is as important to the programme as the food itself.

DELIVERY DETAILS
⏰

Delivered daily including weekends, arriving within the agreed 30-minute window

👤

Same volunteer, same time, every day — consistency is the welfare foundation

🤝

Minimum 10-minute welfare visit — the volunteer is trained to notice and report

🌍

Culturally appropriate, soft food option available, allergen-accommodated

🏥

Quarterly nutrition review with healthcare partner for long-term recipients

👨‍👩‍👧 Families

Weekly family food boxes for food-insecure households — supplementing, not replacing, what families can provide, while building the cooking skills and growing capacity that reduce need over time.

DELIVERY DETAILS
📦

Monthly food security kit — whole food staples, fresh produce, seeds, recipe cards

🚗

Home delivery or hub collection — family chooses what works for their situation

👩‍🍳

Monthly follow-up nutrition coaching call (15 minutes) from ZHIP-trained advisor

📚

Invitation to all hub programmes — kitchen, Academy, growing, community events

📈

Quarterly family food sovereignty review — tracking progress toward self-sufficiency

Food safety and quality

Every meal meets the standard. No exceptions.

Temperature and food safety protocols

🌡️

All hot food leaves the kitchen at or above 75°C (167°F) — the safe cooking temperature for delivery

⏱️

All deliveries completed within 90 minutes of leaving the kitchen — within the safe temperature window

📋

Temperature log completed at packing and verified by kitchen lead before any driver departs

🥡

Insulated delivery containers used for all routes — maintaining food temperature throughout the delivery window

🧼

Containers returned to the hub for commercial-grade cleaning after every use

📱

Any failed delivery (no answer, welfare concern) logged immediately and escalated to hub coordinator

Allergen and dietary management

🏷️

Every meal container labelled with recipient name, date, allergens present, and any dietary notes

📋

Master allergen register maintained for all recipients — updated at every intake review

🌍

Cultural dietary requirements accommodated: halal, kosher, vegetarian, vegan, low-sodium, diabetic-friendly, soft food

🚨

Allergen-free meals prepared in a dedicated area of the hub kitchen to prevent cross-contamination

👩‍🍳

Kitchen lead reviews allergen compliance before every delivery route departs

📞

Emergency protocol for allergic reaction during or after delivery — driver trained and hub coordinator on call

Volunteer driver network

The people who make every delivery possible.

The delivery driver network is the logistical backbone of the entire meal delivery programme. Without reliable volunteer drivers, the best kitchen in the world cannot feed anyone beyond walking distance.

ZHIP coordinates drivers through the hub management platform — matching available drivers to routes, scheduling recurring commitments, handling last-minute cover when a regular volunteer is unavailable, and tracking completed deliveries for impact reporting.

What drivers do

🚗

Collect assigned meals from the hub kitchen in insulated bags at the designated departure time

🗺️

Follow the assigned route — typically 3–6 deliveries per session, within a defined geographic area

🤝

Complete the welfare visit protocol at every door — delivery is not complete until the recipient is checked on

📱

Log every delivery in the ZHIP driver app — receipt confirmed, visit notes, any concerns flagged

🔄

Return containers to the hub at the end of the route for cleaning

Driver requirements and support

✅

Valid driving licence and access to a vehicle with space for a cooler bag

✅

Commitment to a consistent weekly schedule — the recipients rely on knowing who is coming

✅

Two-hour induction covering route management, welfare protocols, the ZHIP driver app, and safeguarding basics

✅

A DBS/background check for routes serving children or vulnerable adults

✅

Mileage reimbursement available for drivers who need it — ZHIP does not expect volunteers to subsidise the programme with their own fuel costs

🏅

ZHIP volunteer certification after 50 hours — formally recognising the contribution drivers make

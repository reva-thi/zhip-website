Programme 4

Community Growing
& Seed Libraries

Growing food changes a community's relationship with food more profoundly than any other single intervention. When you grow a tomato, you understand what food costs in labour, water, and care. When you harvest it and cook it and eat it, you understand what food is worth. ZHIP helps communities grow — at any scale, from a window sill to a shared field.

Three scales of growing

Everyone can grow something.

🪴

Window sill and container growing

For anyone with a window sill, a balcony, or a small outdoor space. Herbs, salad leaves, spring onions, radishes, and cherry tomatoes can all be grown in containers. ZHIP provides starter kits and digital growing guides for container growers.

Space needed: A window sill. A balcony. A porch step.

🌿

Household or shared garden plots

For households with garden space, or groups of neighbours sharing a back yard or rented plot. ZHIP's growing coaches help design productive raised beds, manage soil health, plan the growing calendar, and get the most from the space available.

Space needed: One raised bed (1m x 2m) produces meaningful food.

🌾

Community growing sites

Larger shared growing spaces — managed collectively by a group of families or a community organisation. ZHIP supports site design, community governance of the growing space, seed library setup, composting infrastructure, and connection of the harvest to the community kitchen.

Space needed: Any land that can be accessed and cultivated together.

What is a Seed Library?

A living resource that grows every year.

A ZHIP Seed Library is a community-owned collection of seeds — stored, managed, and grown by community members — that anyone can borrow from, grow, and return to after harvest. It is based on an ancient principle: seeds are a commons, not a commodity. They belong to everyone.

The library starts with a founding collection of 50 or more seed varieties — chosen with the specific community to include seeds relevant to its food culture and heritage. As community members borrow seeds, grow them, and return seeds saved from their harvest, the library grows larger and more diverse every year.

How the borrow-grow-return cycle works

📦

Borrow — a community member visits the seed library and selects seeds for the current season. They sign out the varieties they are taking, along with a growing guide and seed-saving instructions.

🌱

Grow — the member grows the plants at home or on a community plot. The growing coach is available for advice throughout the season.

🫙

Save — at the end of the season, the member saves seeds from the best specimens of what they grew — selecting for the plants that thrived in their specific local conditions.

🔄

Return — they return their saved seeds to the library — labelled, dried, and ready for the next borrower. They usually return more seeds than they borrowed, adding to the library's stock.

Starting a growing programme

What ZHIP provides. What you provide.

What ZHIP provides

📋

Site assessment guide — a simple tool for evaluating any space's growing potential (sunlight, water access, soil, security, accessibility)

🫘

Founding seed collection — 50+ varieties chosen with your community, including heritage and culturally significant varieties

♻️

Composting system setup — two-bin composting infrastructure, instruction guide, and assignment of a composting champion

📅

Seasonal growing calendar — adapted for your local climate and the specific crops your community prioritises

👨‍🌾

Growing coach training — your growing coach receives hands-on training in organic growing, seed saving, and community garden management

📊

Harvest tracking tools — log what is grown, where it goes (community kitchen, families, market), and how the growing programme develops over time

What your community provides

🏡

Access to growing space — even small or challenging spaces can be made productive with the right approach

👥

A committed growing group — even four or five families committed to showing up on Saturday mornings can sustain a meaningful growing programme

🌍

Traditional growing knowledge — elders in most communities hold growing wisdom that no manual can replicate. Bringing that knowledge into the programme makes it richer and more culturally rooted.

💧

Consistent care — plants need watering, weeding, and attention. Growing programmes thrive on regularity and gentle collective attention, not occasional heroic effort.

🤝

A willingness to share — the harvest is a community resource. The culture of sharing what is grown — with each other, with the community kitchen, with neighbours who cannot grow — is the foundation of the whole programme.

Grow something. Change everything.

Start with one seed. One pot. One season. The relationship with food that follows will last a lifetime.

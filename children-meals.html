Children's Nutrition First

No child goes
hungry on our watch.

Hot school meals, holiday food clubs, malnutrition intervention, daily meal delivery for homebound children, and youth nutrition programmes — because childhood hunger is not just about food. It is about cognitive development, educational attainment, emotional health, and the kind of adult a child becomes.

The reality of childhood hunger

What hunger does to a child's development.

🧠

Cognitive impact

A hungry child cannot concentrate. Research consistently shows that children who skip breakfast perform significantly worse on tests of attention, memory, and problem-solving — regardless of their baseline intelligence. Chronic hunger in early childhood permanently affects brain development.

📚

Educational attainment

Food-insecure children are more likely to be held back a year, more likely to experience behavioural difficulties in school, and less likely to graduate high school. The correlation between childhood food security and educational outcomes is one of the strongest in child development research.

💔

Emotional and social development

Children who are food insecure experience higher rates of anxiety, depression, and social withdrawal. The shame of not having enough food — particularly when peers can see it — has documented long-term effects on self-esteem and social confidence.

Our Children's Nutrition Programmes

Five programmes addressing childhood hunger at every point.

PROGRAMME 1

School Breakfast and Lunch

In partnership with schools in food-insecure areas, ZHIP funds and delivers daily hot breakfast and lunch — freshly cooked, nutritionally balanced, and culturally appropriate. Every child in the school eats. Not just children whose parents fill in the free school meals form. Every child.

The meals are prepared at the ZHIP community kitchen and delivered to the school in insulated containers, or cooked on-site in the school kitchen by ZHIP-trained school kitchen staff. Menus rotate weekly, include at least two portions of vegetables per meal, and reflect the cultural backgrounds of the school community.

What the programme covers

🌅

Hot breakfast served from 7:30am — before the school day starts, available to any child who arrives early

☀️

Hot lunch served at midday — nutritionally complete, culturally appropriate, cooked fresh that morning

🍎

Afternoon snack for children in after-school programmes — fruit, seed mix, or a small wholesome item

A school week in this programme

MONDAY

Breakfast: Oat porridge with banana and seeds. Lunch: Lentil and vegetable soup with whole grain bread.

TUESDAY

Breakfast: Egg and wholegrain toast. Lunch: Chicken and rice (or tofu alternative) with green salad.

WEDNESDAY

Breakfast: Yoghurt with fruit. Lunch: Black bean tacos with fresh salsa and corn.

THURSDAY

Breakfast: Smoothie and whole grain muffin. Lunch: Dal and brown rice with roasted vegetables.

FRIDAY

Breakfast: Banana pancakes. Lunch: West African groundnut stew with rice — the Friday favourite.

Holiday Club — A Typical Day

9am

Children arrive. Hot breakfast is served. A relaxed start — conversation, drawing, catching up with friends they have not seen since the school term ended.

10am

Activity: this week is fermentation week. Children make their own kimchi with adult help. Questions, laughter, careful tasting of ingredients at each stage.

12pm

Hot lunch — the children eat together. The food is good. Nobody is in a hurry.

1pm

Growing session: children water their plot in the community garden and harvest this week's lettuce and tomatoes for tomorrow's lunch.

3pm

Children go home with the jar of kimchi they made, a recipe card, and a seed packet. They have eaten two nutritious meals and learned something that will last.

PROGRAMME 2

Holiday Food Club

Children who depend on school meals do not stop being hungry when school closes. During every school holiday period — summer, Christmas, spring break — ZHIP runs the Holiday Food Club: a daily programme providing hot meals, a growing or cooking activity, and human connection for children who would otherwise be at home without adequate food.

The holiday club is deliberately not just a meal programme. It is a joyful community of children who cook together, grow together, and learn together — in a safe, warm, welcoming space that many food-insecure children need as much as the food itself.

PROGRAMME 3

Child Malnutrition Intervention

Beyond food insecurity — the clinical reality of child malnutrition requires targeted intervention. ZHIP operates in partnership with healthcare providers to identify, assess, and support children showing signs of malnutrition or nutritional deficiency.

The intervention programme is not punitive or bureaucratic. It begins with a home visit, a compassionate conversation with the family, and a practical plan — food security kit delivery, cooking support, healthcare referral, and ongoing monitoring.

What the intervention programme provides

🏥

Clinical assessment — a healthcare partner screens referred children for signs of malnutrition: growth monitoring, dietary history, development assessment

🥗

Therapeutic nutrition plan — a ZHIP nutritionist and the healthcare partner create a personalised nutrition plan for the child and the family

📦

Priority food kit delivery — the family receives a specialist food security kit containing the specific nutrients the child needs, delivered weekly

👩‍🍳

Family cooking support — a ZHIP trainer visits the home to show parents how to prepare the recommended foods in familiar and appealing ways

📊

Progress monitoring — monthly growth and development checks, adjusting the nutrition plan as the child responds to intervention

🤝

Family support referrals — connecting families to all other ZHIP programmes and relevant community services, addressing the root causes of the child's food insecurity

Signs that may indicate a child needs support

Healthcare providers, teachers, and community members can refer children who show any of the following:

⚠️

Visible weight loss or failure to thrive

⚠️

Lethargy, difficulty concentrating, or unusual fatigue

⚠️

Frequent illness or slow recovery from illness

⚠️

Eating very little at school — or hoarding food

⚠️

Parents or caregivers who appear to be struggling with food access or preparation

Referrals are handled with complete confidentiality and without judgment. The aim is always to support families, not to scrutinise or penalise them.

PROGRAMME 4

Youth Nutrition and Food Skills

Teenagers and young adults aged 14–25 are often overlooked in children's food programmes — too old for holiday clubs, not yet independent enough to navigate food assistance systems. ZHIP's youth nutrition programme meets them where they are.

🍳

Youth cooking cohorts — Academy Growers-level cooking courses specifically for young people, designed around the food they actually want to eat, with the skills they will actually use when they leave home

💼

Youth food enterprise incubation — young people interested in food as a career or business are supported to develop their first food enterprise through the Academy enterprise module and Eatzpay micro-financing

🏡

Independent living food support — young people leaving care or moving into independent living receive a three-month food security package and a cooking skills programme designed for solo living on a tight budget

🌱

Youth growing leaders — young people who complete the growing module are trained to lead growing sessions for younger children — building leadership skills while keeping the knowledge in the community

The home meal delivery programme for children

For children and young people who are homebound — due to disability, long-term illness, or caring responsibilities — ZHIP operates a specialist home meal delivery programme parallel to the Senior Meals on Wheels service.

🚗

Daily hot meal delivery to the child's home — arriving at a consistent time that fits the family's routine

👧

Meals designed to be age-appropriate, culturally familiar, and calibrated to the child's specific nutritional needs

🤝

Delivery volunteer who connects with the child — not just drops food at the door — providing human contact and welfare check

📦

Weekly supplementary food box with ingredients, recipe cards, and activity kits so the child can engage with food and cooking even at home

Elder Nourishment Programme

Senior Meals
on Wheels

Hot, nutritious meals delivered daily to seniors who cannot leave their homes — with a human visit alongside every delivery, because the meal and the company matter equally. Nutrition for the body. Connection for the soul.

Enrol a Senior Become a Delivery Volunteer
🍲

Daily hot meal delivery

A nutritious hot meal delivered to the senior's door every day, seven days a week — including weekends and public holidays. No gaps. No exceptions. Seniors can count on it.

🤝

Human connection every visit

Each delivery includes a brief visit from the volunteer — a check-in, a conversation, a moment of human contact. For many isolated seniors, this is the only face they see in a day.

🌍

Culturally appropriate meals

Menus adapted to the cultural food preferences of each senior — because a lifetime of eating certain foods does not disappear in old age, and familiar food is comfort as well as nutrition.

How the programme works

From referral to daily delivery — every step explained.

1

Referral and intake

Seniors are referred to the programme through their doctor, a social worker, a faith community, a family member, a neighbour, or by self-referral. A ZHIP intake coordinator makes a home visit — to meet the senior, understand their dietary needs, medical dietary restrictions, cultural food preferences, and living situation. No means test. Eligibility is simple: the senior needs meals, and they cannot reliably prepare them.

2

Meal planning and preparation

The hub nutritionist or food lead creates a personalised meal plan for each senior — balancing nutritional adequacy with cultural appropriateness and any medical dietary requirements (diabetic-friendly, low-sodium, soft food for dental issues). Meals are prepared fresh in the hub kitchen by the morning volunteer team, portioned individually, labelled with the senior's name, and packed in insulated containers.

3

Matching with a volunteer

Each senior is matched with a consistent delivery volunteer — the same person, the same time each day. Consistency matters enormously: the senior knows who is coming, and the volunteer knows the senior. The relationship that forms over months of daily visits is one of the most valuable outcomes of the programme — not incidental to it.

4

Daily delivery and visit

The volunteer arrives within a 30-minute window — pre-agreed with the senior. They hand over the meal, ask how the senior is feeling, notice anything that seems different from the day before (a fall, an illness, confusion), and stay for the agreed visit time — minimum ten minutes. They record their visit in the ZHIP app, flagging any welfare concerns for the hub coordinator to follow up.

5

Welfare monitoring and referral

Delivery volunteers are trained in basic welfare observation — signs of deterioration, confusion, falls, social distress, medication concerns. Any concern flagged in the app triggers a follow-up by the hub coordinator within 24 hours. Serious concerns are referred to healthcare, social services, or emergency contacts. The programme operates as a daily welfare check as well as a meal delivery.

6

Weekly communal lunch

Once a week, seniors who are able to travel join a communal elder lunch at the hub — a social event as much as a meal. Transport is arranged for those who need it. The communal lunch is the highlight of many seniors' weeks: a table of peers, food to share, and conversation that goes far beyond what a daily door delivery can provide.

The nutrition standard

Every ZHIP Senior Meals on Wheels delivery meets the following nutritional standards, reviewed quarterly by the hub's food and nutrition lead:

✅

Minimum 500 calories per meal — sufficient as a main meal for an elderly adult with reduced appetite

✅

Minimum 25g protein — critical for muscle maintenance and immune function in older adults

✅

At least two portions of vegetables, at least one portion of whole grain carbohydrate

✅

Low sodium by default — adapted to higher sodium for those with no hypertension concerns

✅

Soft food option available for seniors with dental or swallowing difficulties

✅

All allergens identified and accommodated at intake

✅

Cultural dietary practices respected — halal, kosher, vegetarian, vegan as needed

The intergenerational programme

Every senior on the Meals on Wheels programme is invited — on a voluntary basis — to participate in the intergenerational food sessions at the hub: teaching a recipe they know, telling the story of a food tradition they carry, or sitting alongside a young Academy learner and sharing what they know about cooking and growing and feeding people.

Elders are the community's most valuable food knowledge resource. The programme honours that — ensuring that the knowledge they carry does not disappear with them, but is shared, documented, and carried forward by the people they teach.

Who qualifies for Senior Meals on Wheels

✅

Adults aged 60 and above who cannot reliably prepare their own meals

✅

Adults of any age with a disability or health condition that prevents meal preparation

✅

Adults recovering from a hospital stay or surgery who need temporary meal support

✅

Adults experiencing social isolation who would benefit from the daily human visit

There is no income test, no nationality requirement, and no minimum length of commitment. A senior can receive meals for two weeks of recovery or for the rest of their life. The programme meets the person where they are.

Volunteer delivery drivers

The most important journey of your day takes 45 minutes.

The delivery volunteer is the human face of the programme. Every senior knows their volunteer's name. Many seniors say their volunteer is one of the most consistent and caring presences in their life. That relationship is not a side effect of the programme — it is a core objective.

What delivery volunteers do

🚗

Collect meals from the hub kitchen at 11:30am and deliver to 3–5 seniors on their assigned route

🤝

Stay for the agreed visit time (minimum 10 minutes) at each delivery — listen, notice, and connect

📱

Log each delivery in the ZHIP app — confirmed delivery, welfare observations, any concerns

📞

Call the hub coordinator immediately if a senior does not answer the door or appears unwell

📅

Commit to a regular schedule — same days each week — so the senior can rely on them

What delivery volunteers receive

🎓

Two-hour training on delivery protocols, welfare observation, safeguarding, and the ZHIP app

📋

A laminated route card with each senior's address, dietary notes, and any key welfare information

🏅

ZHIP volunteer certification after 50 hours — recognised by employers and professional organisations

🤝

Monthly volunteer network meeting — peer support and shared experience with other delivery volunteers

❤️

The most consistently reported volunteer experience in any ZHIP programme: "This is the best thing I do all week."
